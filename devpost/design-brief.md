# Design brief: Small Wins

<!-- Filled in by plan-lens from the learner's interview. Uses the learner's words. Values they didn't choose are marked (default). -->

## The one spatial moment
You've just closed the dishwasher. You lift your eyes back towards the counter, and in a small patch of empty space on the backsplash, a little plate sticker pops up with sparkles and a personal line: "A clear sink for tomorrow." It settles beside the sticker you earned for the laundry, and for a couple of seconds you can see that this is the second thing you've done for yourself today.

Who it's for: people who struggle with the tiniest things, and whose brain turns a small win into "look at me, the big loser." Only tasks you planned for yourself earn a sticker, so the rewards stay meaningful.

The demo runs in one open-plan (American-style) kitchen and living room: two chores, a reward of me-time on the sofa, then an invitation outside at the door. The demo ends at the door.

## Why it belongs on glasses
- Uses: draws in the world, leaves your body free.
- Why it's better than the phone or laptop version: your hands are full of laundry or dishes, so you don't stop to pull out a phone. The reward appears where you did the task, the breathing guide appears in front of you wherever you've sat down with your tea, and the invitation outside waits by the actual door.

## Product or overlay
- Type: overlay on real life. The virtual rewards respond to real chores in your own home.
- What the real version would sense: that the washing and the dishes are done, and later the real things found on a quest. Real rooms would be real, so none of the furniture would be drawn.
- Stand-in for the preview: a virtual room with demo props. You pinch and drag the laundry into the washing machine and the dishes into the dishwasher, and that drop counts as "task done." The "Raise hand" button and the breathing's "I'm ready" button stand in for the real hand signals. Tapping the door stands in for walking up and opening it. A "Sit on the sofa" button (or tapping the sofa) and a "Stand up" button stand in for sitting, because the preview camera can't sit. In the preview only, the mouse reaches 15 m instead of 5 m, so everything can be tapped from the middle of the room. The view through the door is a simple backdrop, not a world to walk in.

## Where things live
Sizes and distances are real-world scale. The room is about 10 m x 10.6 m with a 3 m ceiling, with bright, warm surfaces so it reads on the additive display. It is this big because the display is only about 36 degrees across: from near the middle of the room, the dishes through to the laundry basket fit in one view, and turning round shows everything else. The demo starts there, facing the kitchen. In real use the room is your own home, so its size doesn't matter.

Look and feel (from the learner's reference photos): an artsy, lived-in flat in a quiet city neighbourhood. Warm white walls so the art and furniture carry the colour, earthy backsplash tiles in quiet tones so stickers stand out against them, oak cabinets with a few painted doors, a terracotta and cream checkerboard floor, a retro yellow fridge, rattan lamps, a green velvet sofa with patterned cushions, a dense gallery wall, a kilim rug and pouf, a rattan dresser, and plenty of plants. Every window and the front door look out on the same neighbourhood.

- Where the user is looking during the main task: down at the appliance and hands, then up and slightly to the right at the backsplash.
- Room layout (default): worktop along one wall with sink, dishwasher and washing machine, and a dining table beside it; sofa against the opposite wall under the gallery, facing the kitchen across the room, with the coffee table and rug in front of it; door to the outside on another wall, directly to your left from the starting spot.
- Doorway to the bedroom: an open, curtained doorway beside the sofa, with a small real room behind it so it has depth as you move. It's set dressing only: you can't walk through it, and nothing happens in there.
- Dirty dishes: piled up in the sink, as several grabbable items (default: 3).
- Laundry basket: in the corner made by the kitchen wall and a tall cupboard, with a pile of clothes, near the washing machine (default: 1.5 m away).
- Sticker hint: a small sticker outline beside the dishes and beside the basket, showing you can earn a sticker there.
- Plate sticker and stars: anchored to the backsplash, slightly right of the dishwasher, just above the worktop. The plate pops to about 20 cm wide, then rests at about 7 cm in the strip. Stars fan out above it, within about 30 cm.
- Message: one line directly below the sticker, in the same patch.
- Sticker strip: same patch, directly below the message. Max 5 stickers shown at a time.
- Laundry reward (default): same layout as the dishes reward, anchored just above the washing machine, with a little clothes-themed sticker.
- Coming into view: rewards, the me-time invitation and the quest card stay where they belong while you can see them. If you're looking away or are too far off to read them, they ease to about a metre in front of you. So from the middle of the room, the reward appears in front of you rather than on the backsplash.
- Me-time invitation: appears where the second reward just faded, or in front of you if that spot is out of view.
- "Find somewhere comfortable" prompt and I'm ready button: stay gently in front of you as you move, low in view and not stuck to your face (default), because you might sit anywhere, even in another room.
- Breathing square: not tied to any place or piece of furniture. It appears wherever you are when you say you're ready: upright, about a metre in front of where you're looking, just below eye level. About the size of a side plate (20 cm, default). Once it appears it stays put, so it doesn't move if you turn to put your tea down. In the demo, the sofa is simply where the learner sits, and sitting there puts you lower and facing the kitchen. Mug of tea on the coffee table as a prop (default).
- Quest sign: on the door at about eye height, big enough to read from across the room.
- Quest card: rides low in view when the door is open, leaving the top of the doorway clear to look through.
- Raise hand: three big choices come up in a row just below the middle of your view, with Me-time on the left, Today's wins in the middle and the To-do list on the right. On real glasses they would sit by your raised hand.
- Notebook cards: one small card at a time, about two thirds of the display wide (33 cm at 70 cm), a little below eye level, and it stays where you opened it. It grows only as tall as what's on it, so it never fills your view.
- Me-time exercises (chosen from the notebook): each opens about where the breathing would, in front of where you're looking, and stays put once it starts.
  - Eye movement calm: a teal band about 1 m in front of you at eye level, with a bright coral dot in a white ring, so it never blends into the room behind it. The dot sweeps about 21 cm each way, so it stays well inside the narrow display and you follow it with your eyes, not your head.
  - Guided drawing: a cream sheet in a teal frame, about the size of an A4 page (34 x 24 cm), about 80 cm in front of you and just below eye level, like paper on a desk. The pen is a dark teal dot in a white ring.
- Text on cards: large enough to read comfortably at a glance on the narrow display, on every card and button. Panels are sized to fit it.
- Buttons: sized for a hand, not a mouse. Every button is at least about 3 degrees of your view (about 4 cm at notebook distance), the three choices by your hand and the exercise choices about 4 degrees, with clear gaps between them, so it's obvious where to point and pinch.
- Jobs around the flat, each with its own place and sticker: a watering can by the palm in the reading corner, a ginger cat with its bowl and a bag of food by the fridge, a mug, plate and bowl left on the dining table, and a phone on the coffee table, in reach from the sofa. The sticker hint shows by each one once it's on today's list, and its sticker pops up right there.
- Raise hand button: in the bottom-left corner of your view wherever you look, clearly a demo control. It steps aside while a reward, the me-time invitation, the breathing, an exercise or the quest card is showing.

## Hands and input
- Laundry: pinch and drag the pile of clothes from the basket into the washing machine, then release.
- Dishes: pinch and drag items from the piled-up sink into the dishwasher, then release. The last one, with the door closing, triggers the reward.
- Me-time: pinch Yes or No, then pinch "I'm ready" once you're sitting comfortably.
- Breathing: pinch Pause or Finish now, at any time.
- Sofa (demo): pinch the sofa or "Sit on the sofa" to sit, and "Stand up" to walk again.
- Door: pinch the door to open it, from anywhere in the room. Walking up to it doesn't open it. Pinch Start, Ignore for today, or Ignore until 5 pm on the quest card.
- Notebook: pinch "Raise hand" (demo button). An animated hand raises with the three choices: Me-time, Today's wins, To-do list. Pinch one to open its card. Each card has a Close button.
- To-do list: tick a task off, Edit it (swap it for another, or remove it), or add one from a short list. Ticking a task off puts the card away and plays its sticker in the room: by the plant, the cat, the table or the phone if it has a place there, or in front of you if it doesn't.
- Today's wins: the day's stickers, big, with what each was for, and the one reduced motion setting.
- Me-time: a short list of exercises to choose from, one big tile each: Box breathing, Eye movement calm (EMDR-inspired) and Guided drawing. Pinch one to start it. Nothing is recommended or pushed; the list is just there when you want it.
- Eye movement calm: pinch Start or Not now. While it runs, Pause and Stop are always there. At the end, pinch how you feel: Calmer, About the same, or Not great.
- Guided drawing: pinch Start or Not now, then "Next step" when you're ready for the next one, "I'm done" on the last, or Finish at any time.
- Jobs around the flat: pinch and carry the watering can to the palm, the bag of food to the cat's bowl, and the mug, plate and bowl from the table to the counter by the sink. Pinch the phone to send the message.
- Targets are big and highlight when aimed at.
- Voice: none.

## When things show
- Sticker hints: show from the start, hide once that task is done.
- Reward sequence: plays once when a task is done, about 5 seconds in all. The sticker pops big with one soft bounce, a gold ring and fourteen twinkling stars. Within about a second it shrinks into its outline in the strip, and the message fades in. Sticker and message rest there for about 3 seconds while the stars fade, then everything fades together. The "Raise hand" button steps aside while this plays. The rest of the room stays clear and still. The sticker is saved even if you look away.
- Reward messages: personal and short. "A clear sink for tomorrow." for dishes. Laundry (default): "Fresh clothes, well done, Szonja." The name is one editable value (default: Szonja).
- Me-time invitation: shows after both chores. It says well done for the dishes and the washing machine, and asks if you want to reward yourself with some me-time: make a tea, sit down, take a few minutes. "Are you ready for some conscious breathing?" Yes / No. Fades on either answer. No leaves the room quiet.
- "Find somewhere comfortable" prompt: shows on Yes, asking you to find somewhere comfortable to sit, in this room or another. It waits as long as you need. Hides when you pinch "I'm ready."
- Breathing square: shows when you pinch "I'm ready," in front of you, with "Follow the dot. Four counts on each side." Then the square stays still, a warm golden dot moves clockwise at a steady speed from the bottom-left corner: up the left edge "Breathe in," across the top "Hold," down the right edge "Breathe out," across the bottom "Rest." Four seconds a side, four rounds, 64 seconds. "Round 1 of 4" inside the square. Translucent teal fill and a thick mint edge. Other prompts fade while it runs.
- End of breathing, or Finish now: the dot comes home, the square fades, "Breathe normally. Take your time." appears with a sticker of three balanced pebbles and a couple of soft sparkles, saved to today's page. Finishing early earns the sticker too. Then it stays quiet.
- Quest hint: when you look towards the door, a star and sign say "A little quest is waiting outside. Tap the door to see it." It hides while the door is open.
- Door and quest card: when you tap the door, it animates open to a view of the outside, a front garden full of yellow flowers with a path to the gate and the quiet street beyond, and the card appears: "Find 3 yellow things," or "3 unique leaves" as another example. The card stays whenever you look at the door until the quest is finished or cancelled on purpose. Ignore for today hides it for the day. Ignore until 5 pm hides it until then, for someone working from home who doesn't need the invitation yet. In the demo it just hides, without waiting for a real clock. Either Ignore, or cancelling, closes the door. Tapping the door also closes it, and it can still be opened afterwards, without the card.
- Notebook: the three choices show only when you raise your hand, and a card only when you pick one. The To-do list holds the tasks you planned: "Do the laundry," "Load the dishwasher," plus the mindfulness moment, up to five. Today's wins shows the day's stickers. Me-time lists the exercises, with a quiet line underneath: "Gentle self-help, not a replacement for therapy." Choosing one closes the card and starts it. Any exercise ticks off the mindfulness moment and earns its own sticker.
- Jobs around the flat (added from the list): water the plants (the can tips, water drops fall, the palm gives a happy sway), feed the cat (the food tips into the bowl, the bowl fills, the bag goes back, and the cat bounces with a little heart), clear the table (each dish settles on the counter), text a friend (the screen lights up and "Sent to Anna: Thinking of you! Coffee soon?" shows above it). Each earns its own sticker with a short line, such as "A full bowl and a very happy cat." Done by hand without being on the list, the job still happens, but only planned tasks earn a sticker, like the chores.
- Eye movement calm (inspired by the eye movements in EMDR, used here only as a calming exercise): an intro says what it is and isn't, "A calming exercise, not trauma therapy. Stop whenever you like." On Start, the first set asks you to think of a place where you feel calm and follow the dot. The dot glides smoothly left and right, about one pass a second, twelve passes a set, four sets. Between sets it hides for about 6 seconds with "Take a slow breath. Notice how you feel." "Set 1 of 4" shows under the track. Stopping early is always fine and still earns the sticker. At the end it asks how you feel. Calmer or About the same gets a kind line. Not great gets "That's okay. Be gentle with yourself. If hard feelings stay, a therapist can help." The reward is a calm-wave sticker.
- Guided drawing (art therapy idea): the intro says "Grab a pen and some paper. Today: a leafy sprig," because on real glasses you'd draw on real paper, following the guide. The sheet shows the whole sprig faintly, then a pen dot traces it a few lines at a time and inks it as it goes: the stem, the lower leaves, the upper leaves, then the veins, about 3 seconds a step, with "Step 1 of 4: ..." saying what to draw. At the end the pen lifts, a soft watercolour wash soaks into the leaves with a few sparkles, "Look what you made!", and the sticker joins the finished drawing. Nothing is marked or judged.
- Reduced motion (default: one on/off setting): the message appears gently and the sticker fades into place, keeping the 2-second hold.
- If something needs attention out of view: rewards and the me-time invitation ease into view, because the display is too narrow to count on you looking at the right patch of wall. The breathing comes to you, wherever you sit, and the door's quest waits until you look at it.

## Done criteria
Each one can be checked in Lens Studio's preview.
1. I can walk and look around an American-style kitchen and living room with a door to the outside, and see the dirty dishes and the laundry basket in the corner, each with a sign that I can earn a sticker.
2. Pinching the clothes into the washing machine, and the dishes into the dishwasher, each plays the pop, stars and personal message, beside that appliance if I'm looking there and in view if I'm not, and the second sticker settles next to the first in the strip.
3. After both tasks I'm asked if I want some me-time. Saying yes asks me to find somewhere comfortable to sit, and I can sit on the sofa, facing the kitchen, and stand up again. Wherever I am, when I pinch "I'm ready" the box breathing appears in front of me, where the dot moves around the square with the changing words. I can end it whenever, and I earn the pebble sticker.
4. When I look at the door I can see there's a quest or sticker to earn. When I tap the door, it animates open to the outside, with the option to start "Find 3 yellow things," ignore for today, or ignore until 5 pm.
5. Pinching "Raise hand" shows an animated hand and three choices: Me-time, Today's wins and To-do list. Each opens one small card. In the list I can tick off, add, swap or remove a task, and Today's wins shows the day's stickers.
6. In Me-time I can choose box breathing, eye movement calm or guided drawing. Eye movement shows what it is and isn't, the dot sweeps across in sets with rests, I can pause or stop at any time, and it asks how I feel. Guided drawing shows the leaf and draws it one line at a time. Each earns its own sticker, and every button is big enough to aim a hand at.
7. I can add jobs around the flat to my list (water the plants, feed the cat, clear the table, text a friend). Each shows its sticker hint where it lives. Doing it there, or ticking it off the list, plays it out and pops its sticker up in the room.

## Out of scope
This version won't do the following. They're the bigger product, for later.
- Recognizing real things: colors, leaves, objects, a real messy flat, or real completed chores.
- The app looking around to suggest tasks (and if it's ever built, opt-in only, because it could feel invasive).
- Recognizing that someone is a new person you're talking to. "Talk to someone new" challenges would rely on you ticking them off yourself, with no identifying of other people.
- Skins and clothes other people can see through their glasses (needs two people and devices).
- Levels, buying skins, and the 5-small-stickers-make-a-bigger-one progression.
- Museum art styles that make the world look like a painting.
- Seeing the real drawing on paper, and step or distance tracking.
- Trauma processing with eye movements. Real EMDR is done with a trained therapist; this app only offers the calming eye movement exercise.
- More exercises that suit glasses, for later: 5-4-3-2-1 grounding (name things you can see around you), muscle relaxation guided by your own hands, a mindful walk with gentle prompts, and a gratitude jar that fills with notes.
- Setup questions and ability levels, 5-a-day suggestions, and morning, daytime and evening breathing routines.
- Remembering stickers or tasks between sessions. Each preview run starts fresh with the same sample day.
- Softening or blurring the real room. The display only adds light, so it can't blur what's really there.
- Walking outside, or doing the quest itself. The demo ends at the door.

## Risks and open questions
- Scope: three activities, a door and a furnished room are near the upper limit for one build. Learner kept this. If the build struggles, simplify the door first, then the room's furniture.
- Virtual room on an additive display: dark surfaces (like the green cabinets in the storyboard) will fade or look see-through. Keep the warm look, but lighten colors until it reads in the preview.
- Panels: the storyboard's dark green panels would look faint. Use brighter teal panels with light text, keeping the style as close as possible.
- Typing in the notebook: a keyboard may not work in the preview. If it doesn't, Add and Edit fall back to picking from a short preset list (default). Remove and tick off still work.
- Sitting down: the preview camera doesn't sit, so a demo button moves you onto the sofa and another stands you up. Seated comfort is "feel unproven."
- Size against the spatial moment: from the middle of the large demo room, the reward appears in front of you rather than on the backsplash, because on the wall it would be tiny. To show the original moment in the video, walk up to the kitchen wall first.
- Raise hand and the "I'm ready" signal: real hand gestures can't be shown in the preview. A "Raise hand" button and an "I'm ready" button stand in, and the video should explain that on real glasses you'd signal with your hand.
- Demo video length: 64 seconds of breathing is too long for the 3-minute limit. The Lens keeps four rounds; show a quarter of a round at regular speed, then speed up or cut it.
- Eye movement safety: self-guided eye movement work can stir up hard memories. Keep it short, slow and framed as calming only, with a calm-place prompt, rests, Stop always in reach, a check-in at the end and a pointer to a therapist. Never suggest it unprompted.
- Wording: this is a supportive self-help idea, not medical treatment. Keep messages kind and never scolding, and describe it that way in the submission (default).

## Build notes for CLAD
- Build for SPECS so it runs and can be demonstrated in Lens Studio's preview. No SPECS device is available.
- Use Spectacles Interaction Kit (SIK) and UI Kit for all interactions, so they work with the mouse in Interactive Preview and with LEAF.
- Build any stand-in exactly as described above, and keep it obvious in the scene.
- Use real-world scale in centimeters. Keep content inside the user's view during the main task.
- The display is additive: black renders as transparent. Don't rely on black or very dark content to be seen; keep text light and readable from where the user stands.
- Get the basic flow working first, then add detail.
- Write LEAF scenarios for each done criterion, run them in preview, and report which pass. Say which criteria have no test.
- Don't record, identify, or follow people.

## After the build: judge it
This part is for you, after CLAD finishes.

1. **Does it work?** Ask your agent which LEAF tests passed and which done criteria have no test.
2. **Walk around it.** Open the preview in Interactive Preview. Click the preview once first. Use WASD to move, Q and E to go down and up, J and L to turn, and I and K to tilt. Clicking acts as a pinch. Stand where a user would and look at the task.
3. **Ask the three questions.**
   - Does it use what I'm looking at?
   - Does it put something in my space, where it belongs?
   - Does it free my hands or let me keep my eyes up?
   - Would it be just as good on a phone? If so, what would make it need the glasses?
4. **Check comfort.**
   - Everything I need is in view while I do the task. Nothing sits at the edges, behind me, or stuck to my face.
   - Every control is within reach of someone standing there.
   - Text reads easily from where I stand. No dark UI disappearing.
   - Things show when needed and get out of the way.
5. **Label what you know.** Write "logic proven" next to anything the tests and preview show working, and "feel unproven" next to anything only real glasses could tell you (comfort over time, real hand tracking, real recognition).
6. **Fix with design notes, not code.** Tell your agent what to change in design terms, for example: "Move the step card 15 cm lower and next to the pan. Make the Next button twice as big. Re-run the tests." If a fix takes under a minute, like nudging a panel, do it yourself in the scene.
7. **Submit.** Record a video under 3 minutes of your Lens in the preview, public on YouTube. Make your repo public with your Lens Studio project and this `devpost/` folder. Details are on the hackathon site: https://learn-specs.devpost.com/resources
